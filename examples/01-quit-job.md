== BRIEF ==
Decide between leaving a current job to work full-time on an early-stage subscription product, or keeping the job and continuing the product alongside it. Known: 8 months of personal savings; 40 paying users at $15/month (about $600/month revenue); growth reported at about 10% per month. Unknown: monthly personal expenses and whether the savings are measured against them; how many months the growth rate covers; churn; where users come from; hours currently spent on the product; whether the job allows reduced hours or leave; dependents or other obligations; ease of re-employment. At stake: income, savings, career continuity, and the product's rate of progress.
Removed framing: "I think it's a great idea", "go all-in"

== CHAIRMAN ==
VERDICT: NO-GO. Keep the job for now, and set a dated test that can turn this into a GO within 30 days.

DECIDED BY: The executor's argument. Quitting can't be undone, but the facts that decide this (churn, real runway, whether the employer allows leave) can be checked in days for almost nothing.

STRONGEST OBJECTION STILL STANDING: All five advisors agreed, so here is the best dissent. Growth may be capped by the hours you can give it, so side-project data understates what full-time work would do. Eight months of savings is a real test budget, and waiting slowly burns the momentum.

WHERE THE COUNCIL DISAGREED:
- Executor vs expansionist, on timing: 7 days versus a 60-90 day sprint.
- Expansionist vs outsider and contrarian, on growth: the expansionist reads 10% a month as compounding signal; the other two call it noise.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS DECISION: This becomes a GO if all four hold:
- Net monthly churn is under 5%.
- Net growth of 8% or more has held for at least 6 months.
- Savings cover at least 6 months of actual expenses.
- Your employer refuses reduced hours or leave.

If all four hold, quit and set a hard stop: if revenue is under $1,500 a month by month 5, go back to a job.

NEXT 3 STEPS:
1. Pull net adds and cancellations for each month from your billing records. You'll know it worked when you have a churn rate and a net growth rate.
2. Track what you actually spend for 30 days and divide your savings by it. You'll know it worked when you have your real number of months of runway.
3. Ask your employer this week for a 4-day week or unpaid leave. You'll know it worked when you have a written yes or no by October 9.
