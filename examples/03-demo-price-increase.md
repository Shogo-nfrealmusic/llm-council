Decide whether to raise a subscription app's price from $9/month to $19/month (a 111% increase), effective next week. Options: raise to $19 as planned; keep $9; or change the approach (e.g. a different amount, different timing, or different treatment of existing vs. new subscribers). At stake: monthly recurring revenue and the existing subscriber base. Unknown: number of subscribers, current churn, what the app does and who uses it, competitor pricing, whether existing subscribers would keep $9, any evidence of willingness to pay $19, costs and margins.
Removed framing: "I'm sure users will pay", "Good idea?"

== CHAIRMAN ==
VERDICT: CHANGE IT. Keep existing subscribers at $9, and charge only new signups $19 from next week, with a price review date written down in advance.

DECIDED BY: The first-principles argument. The brief treats two separate decisions as one: the price for new users and the price for existing users. The contrarian's point about reversibility settled it. A new-signup price can be rolled back, but cancellations from the existing base mostly can't.

STRONGEST OBJECTION STILL STANDING: If most revenue comes from existing subscribers, keeping them at $9 caps growth, and nobody has said how they would ever move off $9. We also still have no evidence that $19 is the right number rather than $12 or $15.

WHERE THE COUNCIL DISAGREED:
- Contrarian vs. the other four on the label (NO-GO vs. CHANGE). But the contrarian's own key point recommends the same grandfathering fix.
- Outsider vs. expansionist on the $19 anchor. The outsider says the number is untested. The expansionist would test even higher prices.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS DECISION: Move existing users to a higher price, with 60 days' notice, only if both hold for 30 days: new-signup conversion at $19 stays at 70% or more of the $9 baseline, and new-subscriber churn at 30 days is no worse than the old cohort's.

NEXT 3 STEPS:
1. Pull the last 4 weeks of signup conversion and monthly churn before switching. You'll know it worked when there is a written baseline number.
2. Launch $19 for new signups only. If traffic allows, split visitors between $15 and $19. You'll know it worked when revenue per visitor is higher than the baseline after 2 weeks.
3. Track weekly subscriber count, not just MRR (the contrarian's warning that the damage can hide behind higher prices). You'll know it worked if existing-subscriber churn stays flat.
