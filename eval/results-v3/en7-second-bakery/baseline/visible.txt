# Not on Monday, and probably not on these terms

Selling out by 2pm shows people want more bread than you're making. It doesn't show that this deal is how you should grow. The numbers are tight.

## The math

**New fixed costs before you sell anything:**
- Rent: $6,000/mo, which is **$360k over 5 years**. Commercial leases for small businesses usually need a personal guarantee too, so check whether yours does.
- Fit-out loan: $60k over 5 years at about 9% is roughly **$1,250/mo**.
- Utilities, insurance and POS: probably another $1,000–1,500/mo.

That's about **$8,500–9,000/mo** before any staff or ingredients.

**Break-even:** your current shop keeps $3k from $22k in sales, a 14% margin. Suppose that after ingredients and labor, about 35–40 cents of each dollar of sales is left to pay fixed costs. Then the new shop needs about **$22–25k/mo in sales just to break even**. That's as much as your current shop sells in total, from a new location with no regulars.

**Your cushion:** new locations usually take 6–12 months to build up sales. If you lose $4–8k a month during that time, your $30k lasts about 4–7 months. After that, the loan and any lease guarantee fall on your personal assets. Meanwhile your attention is split, so the shop that pays the bills could slip too.

## What the 2pm sellout does and doesn't show

- **It shows** unmet demand at your current location.
- **It doesn't show** demand across town, and it doesn't tell you what's limiting you. If the real limit is your production (ovens, bakers, hours), a second storefront doesn't fix it. It needs its own kitchen, or your current kitchen has to supply both. Which is it?

## Cheaper ways to capture that demand first

1. **Raise prices.** Selling out daily means you're probably underpriced. A 10% price increase on $22k is about $2,200/mo, which nearly doubles your profit if volume holds.
2. **Make more at the current shop.** An extra oven, an earlier shift, or pre-orders for your best sellers.
3. **Test the other side of town.** Run a farmers market stall, a pop-up, or wholesale to a café there for 2–3 months, and see what really sells.

## If you still want this location, negotiate before signing

Deadlines like "Monday" are often softer than they sound. Ask for:
- **A shorter first term:** 2–3 years plus renewal options, not 5 fixed.
- **3–6 months rent-free** while you fit out and build up sales.
- **A landlord contribution to the fit-out** (called a tenant improvement allowance).
- **A capped personal guarantee** on the lease, for example 6–12 months' rent, or a clause that lets you walk away if you give notice and hand back the keys cleanly.

## Questions that would change my answer

- Does the $3k/mo profit come after paying yourself a salary, or is it your salary?
- What's your current rent? How does the new location's foot traffic compare?
- Could the new site bake on-site and take over some production from the current shop?

If the landlord gives on terms, a price increase lifts your margin, and a pop-up shows demand across town, a second location could make sense in 6 months. Right now you'd be putting your house on the line to test a guess. Growth is the right goal, but this deal as it stands isn't a safe way to get there.
