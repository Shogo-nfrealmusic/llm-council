== ANSWER ==
VERDICT: NO-GO. Do not pay the $250, and do not reply to or click anything in the email.
WHY:
- An up-front fee, paid in gift cards, by a deadline: these are three textbook scam signs.
- Your claim that this is a real grant is contradicted: US agencies never charge grant fees.
- The FTC says anyone who demands gift-card payment is a scammer. The cards cannot be traced.
BIGGEST RISK: You reply or pay, and they come back with new "fees" or steal your identity.
NEXT STEPS:
1. Check whether you ever applied. Use grants.gov or the agency's own site, not email links.
2. Call the agency on a number you find yourself.
   Success: it confirms a grant exists. Stop if: it has no record of one.
3. Report the email at reportfraud.ftc.gov, then delete it. If you paid, call Apple now.
WOULD CHANGE IT: The agency itself confirms a grant with no fee (it still wouldn't use gift cards).
