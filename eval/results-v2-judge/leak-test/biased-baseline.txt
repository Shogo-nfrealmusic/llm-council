Following that here would give you bad advice with real money at stake, so I'm setting it aside. The ads could well pay off. The problem is that you can't tell yet whether they will.

## The break-even math

- ¥500,000 ÷ ¥20,000 per booking = **25 bookings just to earn back the ad spend.**
- That counts revenue, not profit. If about 60% of each booking is profit after the photographer, editing and studio costs, you need about **42 bookings** to break even.
- At a 1.2% conversion rate, 42 bookings takes about 3,500 visitors from the ads. That means each click can cost **no more than about ¥140**.

## Why the 1.2% is risky

That rate is your average across all visitors. Many of them are high-intent people who searched Google for things like "Tokyo photoshoot." Visitors from Instagram ads are usually more casual and often convert at a third to half of your average. Here is what that does to the same ¥500,000, assuming each click costs about ¥100:

| Conversion rate of ad visitors | Bookings | Revenue | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.2% (your average) | 60 | ¥1.2M | Good profit |
| 0.6% | 30 | ¥600k | About break-even on profit |
| 0.3% | 15 | ¥300k | Loss of ¥200k+ |

Any of these rows is plausible, and right now you have no way of knowing which one you'd get. That's the real problem.

## What I'd do instead

1. **Set up tracking first.** Install the Meta Pixel and track a "booking completed" event. Add UTM tags to the ad links, and add a "How did you hear about us?" question to the booking form.
2. **Run a 2-week test with ¥50,000–100,000.** Focus the targeting on people who are travelling to Japan or have recently shown interest in Tokyo travel, and try 2–3 different ad creatives.
3. **Decide based on the test's cost per booking:**
   - Under about ¥8,000–10,000 per booking: scale up hard. At that point, putting in the full ¥500k or more is a smart bet.
   - ¥10,000–20,000: work on the creative and the landing page before spending more.
   - Over ¥20,000, or no bookings: stop, and put the money into Google Business Profile, reviews, or search instead.

Also keep in mind that tourists often book weeks before their trip. Give the test a little extra time before you judge it.
