== BRIEF ==
Decision: A small tourist photo studio in Tokyo is deciding whether to spend ¥500,000 on Instagram ads next month. The alternatives are not spending it, or spending it in some other way.
Facts: each booking is worth ¥20,000. The website converts 1.2% of visitors into bookings. Cost per booking from ads has never been measured.
Unknown: whether ¥20,000 is revenue or profit per booking, and what the margin is.
Unknown: cost per click or visit from Instagram ads, and whether ad visitors convert at 1.2%.
Unknown: how many bookings the studio can handle next month.
Unknown: what the current booking volume and marketing channels are.
Stake: ¥500,000 in one month.
Removed framing: "I plan to", "Should I do it?"
Mode: quick — 3 advisors, 1 reviewer

== ADVISORS ==

first-principles  CHANGE  75%  Test ¥50k–100k first; CPA is the missing number

Red team: not needed — advisors disagreed

== CHAIRMAN ==
VERDICT: CHANGE IT — Cut ¥500,000 to a ¥50,000–100,000 test tied to confirmed capacity and margin, not full spend.
CRUX: Can the studio actually handle 25–50 extra bookings next month, and is ¥20,000 profit or revenue?
DECIDED BY: first-principles's break-even math — 25 bookings needed at pure profit, 50 at 50% margin, meaning cost ceiling per visit is ¥120–240 — a number nobody has, so ¥500,000 is unjustifiable until a small test produces it.
STRONGEST OBJECTION STILL STANDING: first-principles's own point — tourists book weeks ahead, so judging any one-month test by same-month bookings can make a good campaign look like a failure (or a bad one look fine); the test's readout may itself be unreliable.
WHERE THE COUNCIL DISAGREED:
- contrarian argued NO-GO outright, but leaned on unsupported guesses ("likely" lower conversion, ¥100-200 CPC) instead of derived numbers, weaker than first-principles's explicit math.
- executor and contrarian both treat same-month test results as meaningful; only first-principles flags the trip-date booking lag that could invalidate that assumption — this wasn't resolved by anyone.
- Shared blind spot: all three assume more demand is the constraint; none checked if the studio is already near capacity, in which case no ad spend, tested or not, is worth it.
UNKNOWNS THAT MATTER: (1) Capacity next month — if near-full, this whole spend is moot, pushing to NO-GO. (2) Whether ¥20,000 is profit or revenue and the margin — thin margin roughly doubles the bookings needed, pushing toward smaller/no spend. (3) True cost per ad-driven booking — unmeasured; without it, no ad decision is grounded.
WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS DECISION: Confirmed open capacity for 25+ bookings next month plus a measured cost-per-booking from a small test at or below the ¥120–240/visit ceiling (adjusted for margin) — that would justify scaling toward ¥500,000.
NEXT 3 STEPS:
1. Call studio owner to confirm margin on ¥20,000 and open capacity for next month — success: numbers in hand by Oct 2 / stop if: capacity is already near full, then no ad spend this cycle.
2. Run ¥50,000–100,000 Instagram test, tracking cost per booking and bookings by intended trip date, not just booking date — success: cost per booking at or under margin-adjusted ceiling by Oct 15 / stop if: cost per booking exceeds ¥20,000 gross.
3. Only after steps 1–2 confirm, scale remaining budget proportionally to capacity headroom — success: incremental bookings fit within confirmed slots / stop if: capacity is the binding constraint, not ad spend.
