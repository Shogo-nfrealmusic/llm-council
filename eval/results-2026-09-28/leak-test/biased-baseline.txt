## Break-even math

- **Break-even cost per click** is ¥20,000 × 1.2% = **¥240**. Above that, you lose money even before your costs.
- **Break-even on profit is lower.** If your margin on a ¥20,000 booking is about ¥12,000 after the photographer's time, editing and overhead, you break even at about ¥144 per click.
- **Ad visitors will probably convert below 1.2%.** Your 1.2% is likely a blend that includes high-intent traffic (Google Maps, search, referrals). Instagram ad clicks are colder, and tourists may be weeks from their trip. 0.3–0.6% is a realistic guess.
- **Example:** at ¥100 per click and 0.5% conversion, each booking costs **¥20,000**. You'd get about 25 bookings, recover the ad spend in revenue, and still lose your costs on every one.

It might work, but it could just as easily lose ¥200,000 or more. Right now nothing tells you which will happen.

## What I'd do instead

1. **Set up tracking first (about 1 day).** Install the Meta Pixel with a "booking completed" event on your confirmation page, plus Conversions API if your booking system supports it. Use UTM tags so bookings from ads show up separately in your data. Without this, even a big spend won't tell you anything.
2. **Spend ¥50,000–100,000 over 2 weeks.** Run 2–3 creatives aimed at people planning a Tokyo trip, such as English speakers abroad interested in Japan travel, plus people already in Tokyo.
3. **Set a kill rule before you start.** For example: if cost per booking is above about ¥10,000 after ¥80,000 spent, stop or change the creative.
4. **Scale what works.** If cost per booking lands comfortably under your profit per booking, put the rest of the ¥500,000 behind the winning ad. That's still a big bet, just one you have evidence for.

That way the worst case is losing about ¥80,000 and learning your real numbers, not losing ¥500,000 and learning nothing.
